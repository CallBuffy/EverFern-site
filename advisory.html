The person behind the work

Fast. Serious.
Genuinely human.

Buffy Howard holds a PhD in Leadership Studies and an MBA, but her real education happened in the field: inside struggling organizations, fractured teams and regulatory crises, where the stakes were people's jobs and patients' care. She has also been on the other side of the table. As the owner and operator of a licensed adult family home in Washington State, she knows what it feels like to sign the checks, manage the staff, and be accountable for everything.

She has been described as fast, serious, down to earth and fun. She brings an energy into a room that makes people believe things can actually get better, and then she proves it. She is not a report writer or a slideshow consultant. She walks in, takes charge, and stays until the job is done.

Based in Tacoma, Washington, she works with a small number of organizations at a time and goes all in on each one, because when employees thrive, every other metric follows.

Where it started

Her first job out of college was at a nursing home in the middle of a regulatory crisis. They called it the War Room: sticky notes covering every wall, citations everywhere, everyone scrambling. Buffy walked in, looked around, and knew this was the work she was meant to do.

She earned her administrator license, took over her first facility, poorly run, losing money, with unhappy staff, and turned it around. Then she did it again, and again. For fifteen years she was the person moved from facility to facility once the work was done.

The pattern followed her. As a Regional Vice President at a national urgent care and workers' compensation company, she turned around a failing multi-state region and beat earnings targets by $14 million in one year. At a five-clinic radiation oncology practice, she built the HR, compliance and operational backbone it had never had, while improving culture, patient experience and financial results. As CEO of a multi-site ophthalmology practice, she rebuilt operations from the ground up: new practice management software, payroll, IT and financial controls. Along the way she led value-based care work in alternative payment models, Medicare strategy and payer relationships.

She didn't go looking for broken things. Broken things found her. And she has never left one the way she found it.
